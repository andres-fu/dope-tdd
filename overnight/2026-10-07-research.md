## Sources

1. DORA 2025 "State of AI-assisted Software Development" —
   https://dora.dev/research/2025/dora-report/ (read from repo source and
   https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/announcing-the-2025-dora-report).
   90% use AI at work; 30% report little or no trust in AI-generated code;
   AI adoption correlates positively with throughput and product
   performance and negatively with delivery stability; "AI is an amplifier";
   without strong tests, version control and fast feedback, more change
   volume means instability.

3. DORA metrics guide — https://dora.dev/guides/dora-metrics/ (repo source).

## Findings

What managers want to see (evidence-backed):
- Stability and rework next to throughput: DORA's rework rate and change
  fail rate are the early warning that AI volume is outrunning verification
  (3, 4, 6).
- Review burden and batch size: review turnaround, iteration cycles per
  task, PR size (4, 6, 11).
- Proof that tests are real: mutation score over coverage; proof the agent
  did not delete or weaken tests (6, 12). This is exactly Red First's
  "saw red" and "catches the change".
- A human who reviewed and can explain the diff (12, 4's "verification tax").
- AI vs non-AI comparisons against a team baseline, at team level (6, 10, 11).

What they distrust or ignore:
- Lines of code, tokens, PR counts, suggestion acceptance rate: "vanity",
  "productivity theater" (5, 6, 10).
- Green suites without assertion strength ("perpetually green") (6).
- Self-reported speed on its own: people feel faster while stability drops
  and 30% don't trust the output (1).

Surveillance vs help, design rules that the sources support:
- Aggregate at team/application level, never per-person scores to managers
  (3, 5, 7). Minimum group size before anything shows (10, 7).
- No ranking, no leaderboards; one team's trend over time (3, 5).
- Never a performance-review input; the moment a score enters a review it
  gets negotiated and gamed (9).
- The developer owns the data; sharing is explicit opt-in; the tool states
  what it records (7).
- Tie counts to a practice the team chose (TDD), not generic activity (7).
- Pair counts with one perceptual signal ("did the loop help this week?") (7, 8).
- Prefer evidence and trends to a composite score (9).

## Team view: options

| | 1. Ledger file committed with the code, summarised by a script | 2. Weekly markdown digest, developer-run, pasted by hand | 3. Fleet pane on one machine |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What | Each developer exports `.red-first/ledger.jsonl` (receipt per commit: saw red, caught, unguarded, cycle time); `red-first team` folds every ledger in the repo into `TEAM.md` | `red-first digest` prints one week of a developer's own evidence as markdown; the developer decides whether to post it in the team channel | The manager's Claude Code session reads other developers' ledgers over a shared path or git and draws a pane |
| Privacy | Per-person rows live in git: anyone can diff by author; needs minimum-N aggregation and a per-path opt-out | Fully developer-owned; nothing leaves the laptop unless pasted | Worst: live per-person feed to one screen |
| Manager would open weekly? | Yes if the output is one table in the repo or a CI artefact | Only if developers post it; it reads as "look at my homework" | Yes, but it is a dashboard, which the brief ruled out |
| Effort | Export (needed anyway) + a 100-line fold + a doc on anonymisation | Export + a formatter; smallest | Export + sync + pane; largest |
| Goodhart risk | Medium: numbers in git invite comparison | Low | High |

Recommendation: ship the export (the ledger and the receipt per commit are
core), add option 2 (the digest) because it costs almost nothing and keeps
the developer as the owner, and hold option 1 behind an explicit team opt-in
with minimum-N aggregation (three people, following DX's default) and no
per-author breakdown. Pushback for the owner: every source that studies this
says the harm is individual exposure, and both comparable tools ship with no
reporting at all and are used anyway. A team view is premature for the MVP;
the manager story for now is "the receipts travel with the commits, so a
reviewer or a manager can read them in the PR".

## Team view metrics (if and when it exists)

Show:
1. Share of changes with a receipt that saw red AND caught the change
   (the one number that says the tests are real).
2. Unguarded and never-saw-red counts as a trend, team total only.
3. Median red→green cycle time (a batch-size proxy; long cycles are big batches).
4. Rework: changes to a module within N days of its last green (local proxy
   for DORA rework rate until a deploy signal exists).
5. One perceptual line from the digest: "the loop helped / got in the way"
   (free text or a yes/no).

Leave out, deliberately:
- Per-developer anything (SPACE, DORA, Beck).
- Counts of edits, runs, tool calls, tokens, lines (vanity; Goodhart).
- Test-first ratio as a score (the guard makes it near 100% by construction
  and paused edits hide in it).
- Blocked count (measures friction with the tool, not quality; useful to the
  developer, misleading to a manager).
- Any DORA number the tool cannot actually observe (deploy frequency, CFR,
  recovery time): export the ledger with commit SHAs and let the team's
  existing DORA tooling join on it instead.
